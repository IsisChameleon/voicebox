voicebox · signal chain

One pipeline, two bots, and direction-based party names

pipecat names parties by direction, not identity. Knowing that turns every stage name in this pipeline from confusing into obvious.

Current signal path: input → VAD → STT → TTS → assistant aggregator → output

Why the names invert

pipecat labels parties by direction. Whoever's audio comes in is the "user"; whoever the pipeline speaks as is the "bot" or "assistant".

In voicebox the incoming audio belongs to the app bot under test. The pipeline speaks as the tester, so assistant_aggregator records tester context and handles interruption bookkeeping.

transport.input(), vad, and stt observe the app bot. tts, assistant_aggregator, and transport.output() serve the tester.

Why tester text starts at the top

queue_frames() is pipecat's supported injection point and it enqueues at the source — there is no way to inject partway down.

So the tester's text walks past vad and stt to reach the TTS. Those stages inspect it, match nothing, and forward it untouched.

Where text is produced

voicebox keeps language-model reasoning outside the audio pipeline. The MCP caller chooses the tester's response and passes it to speak().

speak() injects LLMFullResponseStartFrame, LLMTextFrame, and LLMFullResponseEndFrame. TTS consumes their text, and the tester-side assistant aggregator retains the resulting context and interruption state.

Where each event comes from

app_bot_speech_*VAD frames, seen by the pipeline observer. Instant and hardware-independent.
app_bot_transcriptThe TranscriptionFrame, seen by the observer as it leaves stt. Its timestamp uses the earliest unclaimed VAD start; if none exists, the observer logs a privacy-safe warning and uses the current time.
tester_speech_*The tester's playout frames from the output transport.
tester_transcriptEmitted by speak() itself — ground truth, never sent through STT.
